# What is being reconstructed

This is a structured literature survey, not an experiment that deciphers a script. The reconstruction target is the arithmetic and presentation of frozen judgements: 20 historical recovery cases, 22 open/partial/comparator cases, and 26 computational works. The historical sample follows the commissioning brief. It is deliberately selected, contains related/dependent cases (Egyptian/Demotic; the cuneiform sequence), and is not a population of all successful or failed decipherments.

`uv run check.py 2026-11-19-decipherment-methods-and-agents` rebuilds seven expected CSVs with Python's standard library and compares them byte-for-byte. It checks the page's precondition counts, timeline milestones, matrix cells, ranking and language-weight sensitivity, corpus table, slogan counterexamples and inventory totals. It also checks that all coded precondition cells have evidence records, that those records match the case table, and that references resolve to a source ID. It does not independently verify the historical accounts, corpus transcriptions, claimed model results or expert validity of a ranking.

# Named judgement steps

1. **Case and outcome selection.** Treat established productive reading/interpretation as a historical recovery, while acknowledging residual gaps. Hittite is principally a language identification in an already readable script. Demotic shares Egyptian linguistic evidence. Meroitic and Etruscan belong among language gaps, not wholly unread scripts. Linear Elamite and Isthmian proposals remain contested in this survey. Voynich is a contested artefact in the sense that its text's nature is disputed; Vinča is probably not writing and is a comparator, excluded from the priority rank. Add Eteocypriot because a deciphered syllabary can encode another poorly understood language.
2. **Milestone selection.** A year is a selected, labelled event, not the moment a whole script became completely understood. 1822 means the Egyptian announcement; 1841 means the partial Old South Arabian advance; 1878 means Haupt's confirmation of Sumerian as a language; 1909 starts a long Tangut recovery. The year_label and notes preserve the longer process. Different reasonable milestones would alter the timeline but not precondition counts.
3. **Temporal boundary for preconditions.** Historical codes concern the recovery process before/at decisive mapping, not all the evidence known today. Open-case codes concern evidence currently available. `language_known` asks whether the target was independently identifiable with readable linguistic material at the outset; `related_language_known` asks whether a usable known linguistic comparator was brought into the successful test, even when choosing that comparator was itself a discovery. Thus Linear B is `no` for the first and `yes` for the second. The latter is deliberately broader than “a proved relative known before any work”; it includes Greek itself once hypothesised. Do not interpret it as a prospective prior or causal necessity.
4. **Four-state coding.** `yes`: explicit, substantial usable evidence in the cited account. `partial`: short name pairs, contextual/memorial parallels, restricted metalinguistic keys, plausible candidate identity, or a script-type inference developed early in the work. `no`: absent in the relevant account or not established in that sense, not a claim of metaphysical impossibility. `uncertain`: insufficient support to choose. Partial is never silently counted as yes. A full bilingual is a substantial translated parallel or bilingual lexical resource, not merely adjacent inscriptions. Brahmi/Kharosthi coin names and titles and the early Carian Egyptian name pairs are partial keys, not full bilingual texts; Old Persian's initially unread trilingual versions are partial rather than an already translated bilingual. Landa's explanations are partial. Relatedness with a fragmentary relative is partial in open cases, whereas no demonstrated useful relative (Sumerian/Elamite cuneiform) is no. A guessed proper name is not the same as an independent known name; uncertain identifications are partial.
5. **Method reconstruction.** Ordered method steps are paraphrases of the inspected accounts. They describe an iterative research process, not a literal complete notebook or one person's sole work. Citation bundles and source locators accompany each cell. Missing numbers, inaccessible full text and unsourced maxima are explicitly unknown; a source attached to an unknown cell records the scope checked, not evidence that the quantity is unknowable. `decipherment-cell-evidence.csv` identifies agent judgements separately from factual transcriptions/paraphrases. It is not a specialist annotation set.
6. **Corpus normalisation.** Preserve each source's unit, date, inclusion and sign convention. Do not combine inscriptions, objects, fragments, lines, sign tokens, sign types, syllabograms and words. Do not add older and newer estimates. The 1,399 Proto-Elamite texts are a filtered 2019 model selection, not the complete CDLI catalogue. The 724-sign maximum includes numerals. The Indus 417 types belong to M77; 377 actually occur in EBUDS. The Linear A ring's about 119 positions include 35 traces or inferred positions; they are not 119 fully legible signs. Rongorongo's 27 objects are the 2024 paper's inclusion, not an asserted universal consensus. Contemporary corpus counts and many longest-text/token figures remain unverified, clearly marked in the table.
7. **Agent priority scoring.** This ranks opportunities for *checkable narrower gains*, not probabilities of full decipherment. Each of six criteria scores 0–3. One point of disagreement on any criterion is plausible. Scores are authored judgements based on the row's evidence, not measured model performance. No agent loop was run on ancient texts for this snack.

# Priority rubric

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C: corpus | A unique object or unusable fragment | Small/sparse corpus or size not established | Many short records or several substantial ones | Large text collection with enough internal variation for many checks |
| D: digital access | No usable digital evidence located | Images/editions or application-only access | Some machine-readable material, with coverage or rights limitations | Reusable searchable machine-readable core and stated academic reuse terms |
| L: linguistic comparators | None securely usable | Speculative or very fragmentary candidates | Supported family-level comparison | Close well-known comparator, or well-supported language family with usable living comparators |
| A: external anchors | None accepted | Object/find context or repeated internal forms | Names, dates, short parallels or partially understood sign classes | Extended bilingual/dictionary or strong independently interpretable structural constraints |
| T: automatable tests | No defined check | Few weak structural checks | Several comparative/distributional checks | Many feasible inventory, formula, numerical, alignment or rejection tests |
| V: verification | No independent comparable evidence | Weak checks or too few independent passages | Held-out structural/linguistic predictions possible | External labels, dictionary entries or independently constrained arithmetic/records |

Add all six, maximum 18. For ties, higher D first, then higher V, then case ID alphabetically. These tie-breakers provide a reproducible display order, not a meaningful difference in scientific prospects. Vinča receives visible scores but no rank. The alternative doubles L (maximum 21), keeping the tie rules. It moves Proto-Elamite from third to fifth and Zapotec from sixth to fourth; Meroitic moves from fifth to sixth. It shows why full language recovery would produce a different priority order. New substantial texts, a bilingual, a licensed digital corpus, palaeographic corrections or a specialist's rejection of a candidate family could all change scores.

The high A/V values for Proto-Elamite reward arithmetic and document-structure validation, not known phonetic readings. Jurchen's first place concerns completion and correction of a substantially readable system. These qualifications are essential to interpreting the rank.

# Chart and evidence design

Four figures use the exact plotted CSV beside their PNG and Vega-Lite specification: selected milestones; stacked four-state precondition counts; open-case precondition matrix; priority scores. There are no facets, purple/pink marks, implicit CSV parse directives or invented confidence intervals. Numeric columns and encodings are explicit. The chart code renders PNGs with vl-convert-python 1.9.0.post1; the website's release check verifies actual Vega data marks at desktop and phone widths.

The page's historical and open-case tables are shortened from the full case table; all row source links point to the cited account/provider. Provider access and redistribution distinctions are in DATA-SOURCES.md. Corpus texts, sign drawings, book scans, image files and private session records are not redistributed in this public checking package.

# Limits of source reading

Sources were inspected in relevant sections, not all read cover to cover. The provider abstract only was available for Desset et al. 2022, Justeson/Kaufman 1993, Parpola 2011 and Daggumati's 2019 thesis. Some publisher pages failed direct download; indexed primary-source extracts were used with that scope recorded. Pope, Robinson, Coe, Chadwick, Daniels & Bright and Houston's books were not read in full for this survey, are not quoted and have no invented page citations. Daniels's publisher chapter excerpt was read directly. No independent specialist or outside-model review is part of this worker's evidence; the coordinator arranges the subsequent review.
