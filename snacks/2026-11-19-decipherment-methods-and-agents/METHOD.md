# Scope and reconstruction

This is a literature survey, not an ancient decipherment experiment. The selected historical recovery cases are dependent: Egyptian/Demotic share linguistic evidence and several languages share cuneiform. They are not a representative sample of all attempts. The other cases include unread scripts, partial mappings, substantially readable languages with lexical gaps, a contested manuscript and probably non-writing. These categories must not be collapsed into an estimate of full decipherment prospects.

The checker rebuilds seven expected tables using only the standard-library code and frozen CSVs. It checks source identifiers, evidence-to-cell agreement, individual rationales for every historical headline cell and every ordinal score, and comparator matrix/rubric consistency. It does not independently verify history, licensing or an expert's judgement.

# Named judgement steps

1. **Case/category selection.** Retain twenty historical recovery examples. Separate `undeciphered_or_partial` from `readable_with_gaps` in the priority table. Jurchen, Etruscan and Meroitic are in the latter; oracle-bone Chinese character gaps and the largely readable Iberian script/language problem are added there. Tangut remains a historical recovery example; this does not imply all its vocabulary is understood. Vinča is probably not writing and is excluded from ranking.
2. **Temporal boundary.** Historical preconditions concern an explicitly chosen productive stage, not all evidence acquired afterwards. Old Persian uses Grotefend 1802, Linear B Ventris 1952, Maya Knorozov 1952, Carian the Egyptian-name approach, and Luwian the productive 1930s sign-mapping phase. The dates in the timeline mark selected stages and are not all first-ever advances: Tangut 1909 is the dictionary phase, for example. A count over these stage choices is not a test that every first advance required the same evidence. Open-case cells describe current evidence in the inspected sources, whose quantities retain their publication dates.
3. **Readable parallel rule.** `bilingual=yes` requires a substantial independently readable translated parallel or bilingual lexical resource already available at the coded stage. `partial` means restricted name pairs, coin names/titles, metalinguistic sign keys or contextual commemorative parallels. `no` means no such readable parallel/key is established for that stage. Three versions that are all unread are no; a conjectured royal genealogy or toponym is an external anchor, not a partial bilingual. Thus Old Persian and Linear B both receive no. Rödiger's medieval Arabic musnad alphabet keys and Landa's key are partial. The later 1996 Carian bilingual and Karatepe's later extended Luwian confirmation are not backdated; Luwian's early restricted keys are partial. The original full Luwian code was changed in this consistency pass.
4. **Prior language versus comparator.** `language_known` asks about independent linguistic knowledge before recovered readings established the identity. Yes requires readable material of the identified target language, not simply knowledge of languages in its region. Partial means a documented prior continuity/descendant/related-identity clue: Coptic for Egyptian/Demotic, Mayan continuity for Maya, Hebrew/Semitic tradition for Phoenician and Syriac tradition for Palmyrene. These clues do not amount to fully known ancient grammar. No means the identity was established in the recovery, or independent prior identity is not supported in the inspected account. Apply that rule to all twenty cases: Cypriot, Carian and Old Turkic are no, alongside the other identities discovered in the work. This strict code is deliberately separate from `related_language_known`, which asks whether a usable known target or linguistic relative/comparator became available for the successful test, even when choosing it was a discovery. Linear B is no for prior identity and yes for Greek as comparator. Translation-language equivalents belong in the bilingual column; they do not turn Akkadian into a relative of Sumerian or Elamite. Tangut's Chinese/Tibetan linguistic comparison is counted with its Sino-Tibetan relationship, not merely because a translation exists.
5. **Other four-state codes.** Yes, partial, no and uncertain remain separate; partial is never added to yes without labelling. Script-type knowledge means the broad model before sound mapping, allowing partial for a type inferred during preliminary work. Proper names means the selected entry point, not any later use: Maya's pictured common nouns give no, even though later dynastic names mattered greatly. An unestablished name on the Singapore Stone is uncertain, not a securely known anchor. Sumerian/Hittite are language recoveries/extensions within an already shared writing tradition.
6. **Corpus units.** Keep objects, inscriptions, fragments, lines, words, sign tokens, sign types and syllabograms separate. Linear A's older 1,370-document selection has 7,362–7,396 total signs, not that many syllabograms. The 1,399 proto-Elamite texts and 724-sign maximum belong to a filtered 2019 selection. M77/EBUDS figures are dated Indus subsets, not a 2026 census. Unicode code points are not automatically an archaeological sign inventory. The Met's about 13,000 Etruscan texts is a dated 2004 museum summary; the older 9,000 account is retained separately. Missing maxima or compatible contemporary counts remain unknown.
7. **Cell evidence.** Every historical precondition cell and every ordinal score has an individual source-based rationale. Remaining records labelled `row-level source mapping` say exactly: coded from the row's listed sources, not individually annotated. They must not be presented as independently verified cell-by-cell annotations. Source locators/access scopes are separately inspectable. A failed capture is never evidence of a full reading; a successful later retrieval is dated as new access.
8. **Ordinal priorities.** Six author-assigned criteria concern useful, testable gains, not model performance or full-decipherment probability. Scores are based on documented evidence and prospective tests. Corpus access and rights may need resolving before an agent can run. No ancient-text agent benchmark was executed here.

# Rubric, maximum 18

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C corpus | Unique object/unusable fragment | Small/sparse or not audited | Many short records/several substantial texts | Large varied collection |
| D digital access | No usable digital evidence located | Images/editions/application access; preparation needed | Machine-readable material with coverage/rights limits | Searchable reusable core with stated academic reuse terms |
| L linguistic comparator | None securely usable | Speculative/fragmentary candidate | Supported family/target hypothesis | Close known comparator or supported family with usable living comparators |
| A anchors | None accepted | Context/internal repeated forms | Names/dates/short parallels or partial sign classes | Extended dictionary/bilingual or independently interpretable structural constraints |
| T automatable tests | No defined check | Few weak checks | Several checks, constrained by sparsity/noise/access | Multiple feasible corpus-scale inventory/formula/numerical/alignment/rejection tests |
| V independent checking | No comparable evidence | Weak or insufficient independent passages | Predictions on withheld documents/sites/forms | External labels or dictionary entries independent of hypothesis formation |

L and the comparator matrix are constrained in code: 0=no, 1–2=partial, 3=yes. Each score has its own explanation. Rongorongo's available Rapa Nui comparator does not prove that the signs encode Rapa Nui speech. Proto-Elamite's arithmetic counts only in A; V=2 reflects prospective withheld-document tests, never a second arithmetic reward. Etruscan's short nonliteral parallels give A=2 and V=2, not an extensive dictionary. Lower T values recognise that sparse/noisy or unlicensed corpora prevent some proposed loops.

Add the six scores and rank **within** each cohort. Ties use D, then V, then alphabetical case identifier, solely for reproducible display. Equal totals are substantively tied. Vinča has visible scores but no rank. A second calculation doubles L (maximum 21) within each cohort; the ranking CSV gives both ranks. New texts, a reliable bilingual, changed sign identifications, corrected family relationships or licence-audited corpora would change the ranking. This rubric is a judgement and one-point disagreements are reasonable.

# Source access and four distinct tasks

Reading hidden/damaged marks, restoration, known-language translation and decipherment remain separate. Oracle-bone individual-character proposals sit inside a largely understood Chinese tradition; Vesuvius 2025 recovers known Greek text. LingOly is a clue-supplied puzzle benchmark. These are relevant components and tests, not automatic evidence of recovery of an unknown ancient language.

Only named relevant sections were inspected. Several standard books were not read in full and are not quoted. Desset's opening method/provenance sections were retrieved as indexed repository text during this revision, while its direct PDF remained blocked. Matthews 2025 was successfully retrieved and its relevant subsection read during the revision; the original empty capture is still recorded as failed. Yap 2023 remains metadata/search-extract only. Interview sources record named scholars' reported views; those views are attributed rather than converted into proof. The public REVIEW-RESPONSE documents every supplied review finding, including partial applications and rejected implications.
