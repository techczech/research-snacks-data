## The request

### What Dominik asked

How much time do different countries require before someone can work in a profession? Dominik asked whether familiar education routes are treated as normal, or even as the minimum the work needs. He wanted Asian systems in the comparison and the years before university included.

The first comparison covers medicine in six places: California, England, Bavaria in Germany, Japan, India and Singapore. Each row follows one selected domestic route. Bavaria provides a continental European example with an explicit state-level family-practice training rule. California uses the conventional four-year college plus four-year medical degree; Singapore uses NTU's five-year course.

## The answer

### In short

The selected routes reach a general medical licence after **5½ to 9 years from leaving school**. That licence does not mean the same thing as first registration, completion of residency or qualification for independent general practice. The timeline keeps those events separate.

Some differences are substantial. England's five-year medical degree can lead to provisional registration, followed by two foundation years and three years of GP specialty training. California's selected route starts with four years of college before four years of medical school. Its general medical licence can precede completion of family-medicine residency. [GMC](https://www.gmc-uk.org/education/becoming-a-doctor-in-the-uk), [AAMC](https://students-residents.aamc.org/choosing-medical-career/what-consider-you-decide-career-medicine), [Medical Board of California](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Licensing/Physicians-and-Surgeons/Apply/Physicians-and-Surgeons-License/default.aspx)

## The endpoint changes the comparison

### Registration and family-medicine training

All figures below are years after leaving the selected school route. “General licence” means the local general medical licence or full registration; it is not a claim that all holders can practise independently as a GP. The final column identifies what the later marker means. [Recalculated milestone table](data/milestones.csv), [coded stages and sources](data/stages.csv)

| Selected jurisdiction | First registration eligibility | General licence / full registration eligibility | Later GP or family-medicine endpoint |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| California | 8 | 9 | 11: family-medicine residency complete; certification has further conditions. |
| England | 5 | 6 | 10: GP training complete; assessments, CCT and the GP register matter. |
| Bavaria, Germany | 6.25 | 6.25 | 11.25: GP specialist training complete; examination and recognition follow. |
| Japan | 6 | 6 | 11: general-practice specialty training complete; certification requires assessment. |
| India | 4.5 | 5.5 | 8.5: optional MD Family Medicine training complete, with final assessment. |
| Singapore | 5 | 6 | 9: family-medicine residency complete; FPAB accreditation has further conditions. |

California's first marker is its postgraduate training licence. The board requires the PTL or a physician's and surgeon's licence within 180 days of starting residency. The nine-year general-licence marker assumes a US or Canadian graduate with twelve credited months of approved postgraduate training and the required examinations. Initial renewal has a separate 36-month training condition or continuing California residency provision. [California licensing guidance](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Licensing/Physicians-and-Surgeons/default.aspx), [P&S requirements](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Licensing/Physicians-and-Surgeons/Apply/Physicians-and-Surgeons-License/default.aspx)

Japan grants its medical licence before the separate two years of initial clinical training. The later general-practice route is the Japanese Medical Specialty Board's **総合診療** specialty. Singapore's first registration permits PGY1 work; its family-medicine accreditation conditions extend beyond simply spending three years in residency. These are consequential differences in what a marker means. ### What the India originals confirm

The NMC's 2024 curriculum specifies **4½ academic years followed by one year of compulsory rotating medical internship**. The 2021 internship regulation requires at least twelve months and bars permanent registration until the internship requirement is met. The month-54 marker means the academic phase and final examination have been completed; the MBBS degree award follows internship. [CBME 2024, PDF p. 29](https://nmc.org.in/whats-new/download/378), [CRMI 2021, PDF pp. 24–26](https://application.nmc.org.in/storage/nmc_gazette_notifications/231280.pdf), [registration provisions, PDF p. 87](https://nmc.org.in/19GraduateMedicalEducationRegulations1997Amendment04112019-2.pdf)

The original **13 July 2023 NExT notice** defers the examination until further directions. It remains linked by the NMC's current rules index. A bounded check of that index and subsequent news titles found no later commencement notice on 22 November 2026. The timeline therefore adds no operating NExT examination year; it does not predict the rules for a future entering cohort. [Original notice](https://nmc.org.in/whats-new/download/589), [current rules index](https://nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-nmc)

## Limits of this check

### What the years establish

The comparison documents variation in selected requirements. It does not show that shorter routes produce equally competent doctors, or that longer routes are necessary. That would require outcome evidence and a design accounting for selection, curricula, assessments and the patients practitioners treat. No causal minimum is estimated here.

The bars combine conventional routes, standard programme lengths and legal or specialty minima. The source panel names the basis for each stage. They are not typical completion times, and no six-country average is calculated. Admission gaps, service bonds, Singapore National Service, repeats and individual recognition decisions can extend the elapsed route.

### What the school clock assumes

The school clock starts at theoretical **ISCED 1 primary entry**, excluding reception and nursery. The selected school route ends at age 18 in five cases and age 19 for Bavaria's current G9 Gymnasium. Singapore includes junior college, which UIS places after secondary education. World Bank compulsory-schooling duration is retained for context but does not set the university-entry date. [School baselines](data/school-baselines.csv), [UIS workbook cells](data/uis-selected-programmes.csv), [World Bank observations](data/world-bank-school-indicators.csv)

The UIS workbooks mostly describe 2020–21 structures, the selected World Bank observations are from 2025, and professional rules were checked on 22 November 2026. This joins documented structures from different dates; it does not follow one historical cohort. Starting at age 18 rather than medical entry exposes an additional school year in the selected Bavarian route.
